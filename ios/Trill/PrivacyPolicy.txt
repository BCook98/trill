Trill privacy policy
Effective 27 October 2026

bcook develops Trill. The app connects your iPhone or iPad directly to the Trill companion on your computer. Terminal and file connections to your own computer do not pass through a developer-operated relay. Optional agent alerts use the separately configured push relay described below. An optional hosted demo connects to a developer-operated sandbox instead, as described below. The app has no advertising, analytics SDKs, or cross-app tracking.

Data on your device
Trill keeps your computer's name, address, certificate fingerprint, and access credential in this device's Keychain. These details are available only while the device is unlocked and do not sync to other devices. Trill saves terminal preferences locally. It holds terminal screens, project and session details, and unsent drafts in memory while the app runs. After an interruption, Trill does not submit drafts automatically. The app does not intentionally save terminal transcripts on your phone.

Data on your computer
The companion receives authenticated requests, the device name you choose, terminal input, terminal actions, and selected photos. It saves device registrations and pairing details locally. Herdr and the programs you run in the terminal may also save history, prompts, files, and logs. You control their storage and retention on your computer.

Optional agent alerts
Agent alerts are off by default and require a push-enabled build, an explicitly configured companion, and your notification permission. When enabled for a computer, Trill sends its Apple push device token, a random computer identifier, and your attention/completion preferences to that computer. The companion stores this registration privately alongside the pairing. For each alert it sends the token, opaque computer/session/event identifiers, and the alert type to its configured HTTPS relay. The relay sends Apple a fixed generic alert and routing identifiers. Terminal output, prompts, filenames, computer/session display names, and access credentials are not included in the notification payload. The relay implementation does not persist alert content or registrations; its hosting provider can process network metadata under the operator's policies. Review the configured relay operator's privacy information before enabling alerts. This build keeps alerts unavailable until the push service and signing configuration are explicitly enabled.

Turning alerts off deletes the computer's push registration when reachable. Forgetting or replacing a pairing waits for alert-registration cleanup; reconnect or revoke the device on the Mac if cleanup fails. Device revocation also removes the registration. Turning off iOS notification permission is reconciled when Trill next becomes active; registration otherwise stops sending after seven days without renewal or when Apple invalidates the token. Expired registration data is removed on the next pairing-store write. An alert already submitted to Apple cannot be recalled. Local delivered alerts for the computer are removed during cleanup. Uninstalling the app cannot immediately contact the computer for cleanup.

Project files and page previews
When you browse the workspace, Trill requests folder listings and the project files or session photos you open from your Mac, then displays them on your device. Video previews download a temporary protected copy to your device for native playback. Closing the preview or cancelling a download removes the copy. After an unexpected app termination, temporary files may remain until iOS clears them. Browser previews use a WebKit data store that does not persist between browser sessions. The companion forwards local development pages using a separate preview credential that expires. Direct HTML and SVG file previews disable scripts and web network resources, and read only supported local assets from the selected folder. Trill contacts ordinary websites over the network. Pages can run scripts, load resources from other services, and process your interactions under their own policies. Closing the browser releases its preview connection. The servers that host those pages may keep logs.

Coding agents and third-party AI
On your own computer, start coding agents inside Herdr terminals. The agent may send terminal input and files it can access, including uploaded photos, to its AI provider. Your Mac's configuration determines which provider the agent uses and its privacy, training, and retention settings. Trill does not choose the provider or access its account settings. Check the provider in Herdr or the agent on your computer before sending sensitive information. Trill asks for permission before you open a terminal or choose photos to upload. You can choose to remember terminal permission for a paired computer. Photo-upload approval is remembered separately for that pairing. These choices apply until you reset them in Connection details, forget the computer, or pair it again. A change of address, access credential, or pinned certificate requires permission again. Terminal and photo-upload permissions remain separate. You can cancel any of these actions or disconnect to stop further input from Trill. The agent or provider controls data it has already received under its own policies and deletion controls.

Photos and camera
The system photo picker gives Trill access only to images you select. After you give permission, choosing an image immediately uploads a JPEG copy to companion storage on the connected computer. On your own Mac, this storage is private to its user account; the hosted demo is shared. Trill reduces the longest edge to at most 2048 pixels and re-encodes the image without location or EXIF metadata. Closing the draft or forgetting the computer does not delete the uploaded copy. On your own Mac, delete it from companion storage when you no longer need it. Hosted-demo retention and deletion are described below. Inserting the file path does not press Return. The remote program decides how it reads files and input. Trill uses the camera only to scan pairing codes. Apple's system keyboard handles dictation under your device settings; Trill does not record microphone audio.

Networks and external links
Trill uses HTTPS for companion API requests and terminal streams. On a local network, traffic goes directly to your computer. If you use Tailscale or another VPN, that service handles network traffic under its own policies and configuration. Opening a terminal link in a browser contacts that website under its policies.

Retention, deletion, and your choices
Forget computer removes the primary pairing credential from this device and closes Trill's connections. You can discard an unfinished pairing separately on the pairing screen. Forgetting the computer or uninstalling Trill does not revoke its device registration on your Mac, delete uploaded files or Herdr history, or stop sessions. Run "trill devices" and "trill revoke DEVICE_ID" on the Mac to revoke access. Delete uploaded photos from ~/.local/share/trill/attachments or the custom attachment directory you configured. Delete session data through Herdr and the programs or providers you used. You can deny camera access and paste a pairing link instead. To stop further sharing, cancel an action, disconnect, or forget the computer.

Optional hosted demo
The developer operates a shared demo sandbox on Fly.io so testers can try Trill without connecting their own computer. If you pair with this sandbox, it receives your chosen device name, pairing and access details, terminal input, terminal actions, and selected photos. It stores device registrations, demo session content, generated files, and uploaded photos. The developer can access this server data, and other paired testers can access demo sessions and their files. Use sample text and photos only; do not send private code, credentials, or personal content. The demo uses real HTTPS terminal connections, but its sessions are scripted: no Herdr, shell, coding agent, or AI provider runs there. The sandbox resets its stored demo data after 24 hours of inactivity across the whole sandbox. Other testers' activity and active terminal connections can extend this period, so this is not a 24-hour deletion deadline for each upload. Forgetting the demo in Trill does not delete server data or revoke its device registration. Email trill@bcook.io to request deletion, without sending credentials or private content. Fly.io may process network and request details to host and secure the service under its own policies.

Hosted service diagnostics
The hosted demo also keeps session actions and timestamps, terminal dimensions, and pairing registration identifiers to provide its features and manage access. This information can be associated with a paired device or session. Fly.io documents seven-day retention for application logs and about 15 days for aggregate infrastructure metrics. Its proxy error logs can include hostnames and request paths, which may contain session or connection identifiers. These records support troubleshooting, security, and service availability. Fly's logs and metrics are separate from the demo's 24-hour inactivity reset. They are not used by Trill for advertising or tracking.

Apple crash reports
The developer uses crash reports from Apple's developer tools to diagnose and fix crashes. Reports can include device identifiers, app and OS versions, crash timestamps, and technical details. TestFlight feedback can also include tester contact details and comments. The developer does not use these reports for advertising or tracking.

Support and website
If you email trill@bcook.io, the developer receives your email address, message, and any attachments you send. The developer uses this correspondence to respond and keeps it as needed to resolve your request and maintain support records. You can request deletion by email, subject to legal retention requirements. Leave out credentials, pairing codes, terminal secrets, and private project content. Website hosting providers may process request details such as your IP address, browser information, and request time to deliver and secure trill.bcook.io. The website has no advertising or analytics scripts, signup forms, or tracking cookies.

Children and changes
Trill is a developer tool and is not intended for children under 13. The developer does not knowingly collect personal information from children. When features or data practices change, the developer will update this policy and the effective date above.
